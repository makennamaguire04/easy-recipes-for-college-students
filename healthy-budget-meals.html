Eat well without overspending

Cheap, healthy college meals

Eating well in college doesn't require expensive ingredients or complicated recipes. Build filling meals around affordable staples, add fruits and vegetables, and choose flexible recipes that make the most of your grocery budget.

Our rule: Keep it simple, use what you have, and make ingredients work across multiple meals.

Recipe 01 · Meat-free favorite

Hearty Bean & Veggie Chili

30 minutes · 4 servings

A comforting one-pot dinner made with pantry staples. Serve it on its own or spoon it over rice or a baked potato.

Ingredients

  • 1 can black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 can kidney beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 can diced tomatoes
  • 1 cup frozen corn
  • 1/2 onion, diced
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 teaspoon chili powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 1 cup water or low-sodium vegetable broth

Instructions

  1. Heat oil in a pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook for 3–4 minutes until softened.
  2. Stir in chili powder and cumin. Cook for about 30 seconds until fragrant.
  3. Add both beans, diced tomatoes, corn, and water or broth.
  4. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook uncovered for 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  5. Taste and adjust the seasoning. Serve with rice, a spoonful of yogurt, or a little shredded cheese if desired.
Budget tip: Make a double batch if you have freezer space. Refrigerate portions you will eat within 3–4 days and freeze the rest.
Recipe 02 · Breakfast or lunch

Veggie & Egg Breakfast Wrap

10 minutes · 1 serving

A filling wrap for mornings when you need breakfast to travel with you. The ingredients are easy to adjust based on what's in your fridge.

Ingredients

  • 1 large tortilla
  • 2 eggs
  • 1/2 cup frozen spinach or mixed vegetables
  • 2 tablespoons shredded cheese
  • 1 teaspoon oil or butter
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Salsa, optional

Instructions

  1. Heat oil in a skillet over medium heat. Cook the frozen vegetables until hot and any excess moisture has evaporated.
  2. Beat the eggs with salt and pepper, pour them into the skillet, and scramble until fully cooked.
  3. Sprinkle the cheese over the eggs and let it melt.
  4. Spoon the mixture onto the tortilla, add salsa if desired, fold in the sides, and roll into a wrap.
  5. Eat immediately or cool the filling promptly before storing it in the refrigerator for a later meal.
Budget tip: Use leftover roasted vegetables, or swap in black beans for a different filling.
Recipe 03 · Easy dinner

Garlic Chickpea Pasta

20 minutes · 2 servings

Comfort-food pasta with chickpeas for added protein and spinach for a simple vegetable boost.

Ingredients

  • 6 ounces pasta
  • 1 can chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 cup frozen spinach
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1/4 teaspoon chili flakes, optional
  • 1/4 cup grated Parmesan, optional
  • Salt and black pepper

Instructions

  1. Cook the pasta according to the package directions. Reserve 1/2 cup of pasta water before draining.
  2. While the pasta cooks, warm olive oil in a skillet over medium-low heat. Add the garlic and chili flakes, if using, and cook briefly without browning the garlic.
  3. Add the chickpeas and frozen spinach. Cook until the spinach is hot and the chickpeas are warmed through.
  4. Add the drained pasta and a splash of reserved pasta water. Toss until everything is coated.
  5. Season with salt and pepper. Finish with Parmesan if desired.
Budget tip: Buy store-brand pasta and canned chickpeas. Use any leafy greens you already have instead of buying a new ingredient.
Shop smarter, waste less

Five ways to stretch your grocery budget

  1. Plan around sales. Choose recipes based on affordable produce and proteins that week.
  2. Compare unit prices. Check the price per ounce or pound instead of relying on package size alone.
  3. Reuse ingredients. Spinach can go into wraps, pasta, and rice bowls; beans can become chili, tacos, or salads.
  4. Use frozen produce. It can reduce spoilage and lets you use only what you need.
  5. Make a list and stick to it. Check your cupboards first and avoid buying specialty ingredients for just one meal.

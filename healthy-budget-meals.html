Cheap, Healthy College Meals

Eating well in college doesn't require expensive ingredients or complicated recipes. These ten affordable meals use everyday grocery staples to help you make satisfying breakfasts, lunches, and dinners on a student budget.

Recipe 01

Hearty Bean and Veggie Chili

30 minutes · 4 servings

A comforting one-pot meal made with affordable pantry staples.

Ingredients

  • 1 can black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 can kidney beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 can diced tomatoes
  • 1 cup corn
  • 1/2 onion, diced
  • 1 teaspoon chili powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • 1 cup water or vegetable broth

Instructions

  1. Sauté onion in a pot until softened.
  2. Stir in chili powder and cumin.
  3. Add beans, tomatoes, corn, and water or broth.
  4. Simmer for 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  5. Serve alone or over rice.
Budget tip: Make extra portions and freeze what you won't eat within 3–4 days.
Recipe 02

Veggie and Egg Breakfast Wrap

10 minutes · 1 serving

A filling breakfast that works for busy mornings or an easy lunch.

Ingredients

  • 1 large tortilla
  • 2 eggs
  • 1/2 cup frozen spinach or mixed vegetables
  • 2 tablespoons shredded cheese
  • 1 teaspoon oil
  • Salsa, optional

Instructions

  1. Heat oil in a skillet and cook vegetables until hot.
  2. Beat the eggs and pour them into the skillet.
  3. Scramble until fully cooked, then add cheese.
  4. Spoon onto the tortilla, add salsa, and roll into a wrap.
Budget tip: Use leftover vegetables and whichever cheese is on sale.
Recipe 03

Garlic Chickpea Pasta

20 minutes · 2 servings

A simple pasta dinner with chickpeas and spinach for extra variety.

Ingredients

  • 6 ounces pasta
  • 1 can chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 cup frozen spinach
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • Salt and pepper
  • Grated Parmesan, optional

Instructions

  1. Cook pasta according to package directions and reserve a little pasta water.
  2. Gently cook garlic in olive oil for about 30 seconds.
  3. Add chickpeas and spinach and heat through.
  4. Toss with drained pasta and a splash of pasta water.
  5. Season and finish with Parmesan if desired.
Budget tip: Store-brand pasta and canned chickpeas make this an easy pantry meal.
Recipe 04

Lentil Tomato Soup

30 minutes · 4 servings

A warming soup with lentils, tomatoes, and vegetables.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup dry lentils, rinsed
  • 1 can diced tomatoes
  • 1 carrot, diced
  • 1/2 onion, diced
  • 4 cups broth or water
  • 1 teaspoon Italian seasoning
  • Salt and pepper

Instructions

  1. Sauté onion and carrot in a pot until beginning to soften.
  2. Add lentils, tomatoes, broth or water, and Italian seasoning.
  3. Bring to a boil, then lower the heat.
  4. Simmer for 20–25 minutes until lentils are tender.
  5. Season to taste and serve with bread if desired.
Budget tip: Dried lentils are versatile and can be used in soups, curries, and salads.
Recipe 05

Peanut Butter Banana Toast

5 minutes · 1 serving

A quick breakfast or snack with a combination of carbohydrates and protein.

Ingredients

  • 2 slices whole-grain bread
  • 1 tablespoon peanut butter
  • 1 banana
  • Cinnamon
  • Plain yogurt, optional

Instructions

  1. Toast the bread to your preferred crispness.
  2. Spread peanut butter over the toast.
  3. Slice the banana and arrange it on top.
  4. Sprinkle with cinnamon and serve with yogurt if desired.
Budget tip: Bananas are often an affordable fruit choice, especially when purchased loose.
Recipe 06

Black Bean and Corn Salad

10 minutes · 2 servings

A fresh, colorful salad that can be eaten on its own or used as a taco filling.

Ingredients

  • 1 can black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 cup corn
  • 1 tomato, chopped
  • 1 tablespoon lime juice
  • 1 teaspoon olive oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon cumin
  • Optional salad greens

Instructions

  1. Combine beans, corn, and chopped tomato in a bowl.
  2. Whisk lime juice, oil, and cumin together.
  3. Pour the dressing over the salad and toss.
  4. Serve over greens or with tortillas or toast.
Budget tip: Frozen corn works just as well as canned corn. Thaw and drain it before using.
Recipe 07

Baked Potato with Broccoli and Beans

15 minutes · 1 serving

A filling meal built around an inexpensive potato.

Ingredients

  • 1 large potato
  • 1 cup broccoli florets
  • 1/2 cup beans, drained and rinsed
  • 2 tablespoons plain yogurt
  • Black pepper
  • Shredded cheese, optional

Instructions

  1. Pierce the potato with a fork and microwave until tender, turning halfway through.
  2. Steam or microwave broccoli until tender.
  3. Warm the beans until hot.
  4. Split the potato and add broccoli, beans, and yogurt.
  5. Season with pepper and add cheese if desired.
Budget tip: Buy potatoes in a bag and use the remaining ones for other meals.
Recipe 08

Cabbage and Egg Stir-Fry

15 minutes · 2 servings

A quick skillet dinner that uses affordable vegetables and eggs.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups shredded cabbage
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 cup cooked rice
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon oil
  • Black pepper

Instructions

  1. Heat oil in a skillet and add the cabbage.
  2. Cook for 4–5 minutes until softened.
  3. Move cabbage to one side and scramble the eggs until fully cooked.
  4. Add cooked rice and soy sauce.
  5. Stir-fry until everything is hot, then season with pepper.
Budget tip: Cabbage is versatile and can also be used in tacos, soups, and salads.
Recipe 09

Tuna and White Bean Salad

10 minutes · 2 servings

A protein-rich pantry meal that works as a salad, sandwich filling, or toast topping.

Ingredients

  • 1 can tuna, drained
  • 1 can white beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • Chopped onion, optional
  • Black pepper
  • Salad greens or bread for serving

Instructions

  1. Combine tuna and white beans in a bowl.
  2. Add lemon juice, olive oil, and chopped onion if using.
  3. Mix gently and season with black pepper.
  4. Serve over greens, on toast, or inside a tortilla.
Budget tip: Keep canned beans and tuna in your pantry for days when you don't have fresh ingredients.
Recipe 10

Carrot and Red Lentil Curry

30 minutes · 4 servings

A simple, comforting curry that pairs well with rice.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup red lentils, rinsed
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 1 can diced tomatoes
  • 1 tablespoon curry powder
  • 3 cups water or broth
  • Cooked rice for serving
  • Salt and pepper

Instructions

  1. Combine lentils, carrots, tomatoes, curry powder, and water or broth in a pot.
  2. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat to a simmer.
  3. Cook for 20–25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until lentils and carrots are tender.
  4. Add a little more water if the curry becomes too thick.
  5. Season and serve over rice.
Budget tip: Use leftover rice and add frozen spinach near the end for extra vegetables.

Five ways to stretch your grocery budget

  1. Plan meals around ingredients you already have.
  2. Compare unit prices at the grocery store.
  3. Choose frozen vegetables when they offer better value.
  4. Reuse ingredients across several meals.
  5. Freeze extra portions before they spoil.

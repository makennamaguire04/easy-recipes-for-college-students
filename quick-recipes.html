Quick, easy recipes for college students

When you're balancing lectures, work, friends, and late-night study sessions, cooking should feel manageable. These three recipes use everyday ingredients and take about 10–20 minutes to make.

Kitchen basics: A skillet, a pot, a microwave, and a few pantry staples will cover most of these meals.

Recipe 01

Cheesy Black Bean Quesadillas

15 minutes · Serves 2

Crispy on the outside, melty in the middle, and easy to customize with whatever vegetables are in your fridge.

Ingredients

  • 2 large flour tortillas
  • 1 cup canned black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 cup shredded cheese
  • 1/2 bell pepper, diced
  • 1/2 teaspoon taco seasoning
  • 1 teaspoon oil
  • Salsa or plain yogurt for serving

Instructions

  1. Mash half the black beans with a fork and mix with the remaining beans, diced pepper, and taco seasoning.
  2. Place a tortilla in a lightly oiled skillet over medium heat. Add half the cheese and half the bean mixture to one side.
  3. Fold the tortilla in half and cook for 2–3 minutes per side until golden and the cheese melts.
  4. Repeat with the second tortilla. Cut into wedges and serve with salsa or plain yogurt.
Student hack: Use frozen peppers or leftover cooked vegetables to reduce prep time and food waste.
Recipe 02

10-Minute Peanut Noodles

10–15 minutes · Serves 2

A creamy, savory noodle bowl with a little sweetness. Add vegetables for extra color and crunch.

Ingredients

  • 6 ounces spaghetti or noodles
  • 2 tablespoons peanut butter
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon honey or maple syrup
  • 1 teaspoon lime juice or vinegar
  • 2–4 tablespoons warm water
  • 1 cup shredded carrots or sliced cucumber

Instructions

  1. Cook noodles according to the package directions. Reserve a little cooking water before draining.
  2. Whisk peanut butter, soy sauce, honey, lime juice, and warm water in a bowl until smooth.
  3. Toss the warm noodles with the sauce, adding a splash of cooking water if needed.
  4. Top with carrots or cucumber. Add chili flakes if you like a little heat.
Student hack: Frozen edamame makes an easy protein addition. Cook it according to the package instructions before adding.
Recipe 03

Easy Egg Fried Rice

15 minutes · Serves 2

The ultimate leftover makeover. Day-old rice works especially well because it is less moist and fries more easily.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups cooked, chilled rice
  • 2 eggs
  • 1 cup frozen mixed vegetables
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 green onion, sliced (optional)

Instructions

  1. Heat half the oil in a skillet over medium heat. Scramble the eggs until fully cooked, then transfer them to a plate.
  2. Add the remaining oil and frozen vegetables. Cook for 3–4 minutes until hot.
  3. Add the chilled rice and soy sauce. Break up any clumps and stir-fry until the rice is steaming hot.
  4. Stir the eggs back in, heat through, and finish with sliced green onion if desired.
Food safety: Refrigerate cooked rice promptly. Reheat leftovers until steaming hot and use refrigerated rice within 3–4 days.

Make quick cooking even easier

  • Keep a short grocery list of ingredients you actually use.
  • Choose recipes that share ingredients so nothing gets forgotten in the fridge.
  • Wash and put away dishes while your food cooks.
  • Keep frozen vegetables on hand for quick, nutritious additions.
